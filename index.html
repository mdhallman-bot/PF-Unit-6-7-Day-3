Phase 4 · 71–90 minutes

4. Is my pathway worth the investment?

You've already checked whether your career could cover monthly living costs. Now ask a different question: Would the extra preparation be worth its extra cost? There is no single “right” path. Use what you know from Days 1–2 and label guesses honestly.

Watch this example first (fictional numbers).

Alex could start working now, or complete an additional certificate. Compared with working now, the certificate costs $6,000 more, and Alex could give up $6,000 in wages while training. Afterward, the certificate job might pay $3,000 more per year.

$6,000 + $6,000 = $12,000 extra investment   →   $12,000 ÷ $3,000/year = 4 years to catch up

This is a simplified pre-tax payback estimate, not a guarantee or a complete ROI calculation. A route that pays the same or less may have no financial payback, but other benefits can still matter.

1 Compare two realistic routes

Your first route is the plan you researched on Day 2. Your alternative is a reasonable path you could choose instead. You are comparing the difference between them, not two entire tuition bills or salaries.

2 Estimate three differences

Use researched figures if possible. The amounts in these menus are fictional examples to help you practice. “Unknown” is a valid choice and will prevent a misleading payback figure.

Compare tuition, fees, tools, and other training costs for your route against the alternative. If your plan costs $8,000 and the alternative costs $2,000, enter $6,000 extra.

This is opportunity cost: money you might have earned in the alternative while taking classes or training. Don't count wages you would still earn. If you can work throughout training, $0 may be reasonable.

Compare expected annual starting earnings for each route. If one pays $44,000 and the other $38,000, the difference is $6,000 per year. This is not your entire salary. A negative difference is possible.

3 Understand your result

Extra investment (A + B)—Extra expenses plus missed wages, using your choices.
Estimated time to earn it back—Extra investment ÷ extra annual earnings (only if earnings are higher).

This estimate ignores taxes, interest, changing wages, and uncertainty. A short payback is helpful evidence—not automatic proof that a path is best. A missing result means you selected “unknown” or haven't entered everything yet.
